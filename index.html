01 / Introduction

About me

My interest in Computer Engineering began when I built my first computer. I was fascinated by how individual components worked together to create a functioning system, and that experience sparked my curiosity about both hardware and software. What began as a single project quickly developed into a broader passion for technology. I started experimenting with Raspberry Pi computers, programming in Unreal Engine, and exploring different programming languages to better understand how computers operate.

Since then, I've gained hands-on experience working in IT help desk support, where I've developed practical troubleshooting, networking, and problem-solving skills. At age 14, I earned my CompTIA Network+ certification, reflecting my early commitment to learning about computer networks and information technology. Over the years, I've also gained programming experience in C++, C, Python, Java, JavaScript, and HTML.

Today, I'm pursuing Computer Engineering at the Georgia Institute of Technology, where I hope to deepen my understanding of computer systems, artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity. I particularly enjoy projects that challenge me to combine different technical disciplines and apply classroom knowledge to real-world problems. Building, testing, and troubleshooting help me learn how the individual parts of a system work together. This portfolio documents that process as I develop my engineering abilities and explore career opportunities.

Currently exploring

Hardware and software integration · Cybersecurity · AI · Embedded systems · Digital design

02 / Direction

Career Goals & ECE Roadmap

My primary career goal is to become a cybersecurity engineer, specializing in penetration testing and the growing role of artificial intelligence in cybersecurity. I'm particularly interested in understanding how vulnerabilities emerge within complex computer systems, how security professionals identify and address them, and how AI can strengthen defenses while also introducing new security challenges.

In addition to pursuing a technical career, I aspire to become an entrepreneur. I hope to combine engineering knowledge with business experience to develop useful technology or eventually establish a company in the technology and cybersecurity industries. This ambition means I need to develop communication and leadership skills alongside technical expertise.

To achieve these goals, I plan to take advantage of Georgia Tech's Computing Hardware and Emerging Architectures (CHEA) and Cybersecurity threads. I also intend to incorporate Computer Science and business electives to strengthen my programming abilities and entrepreneurial knowledge. Outside the classroom, I plan to pursue internships or technical employment every summer and participate in Vertically Integrated Projects (VIPs) and undergraduate research during the school year.

I expect this roadmap to evolve as I gain experience. Coursework is important for building a foundation, but internships, collaborative projects, and research will challenge me to put what I learn into practice. By graduation, I hope to have a portfolio that demonstrates not only what I have studied, but also how I have applied those skills to unfamiliar problems and contributed to real teams.

Learn

CHEA and Cybersecurity threads, Computer Science electives, and business courses.

Practice

Summer internships or work, VIP teams, independent projects, and undergraduate research.

Grow

Cybersecurity engineering, penetration testing, AI security, and entrepreneurship.

05 / Learning in progress

Reflections

Engineering process

Experience beyond grades

One of the most important lessons I have learned is that grades are not the only measure of success in school. Grades can show how well I understand a subject in an academic setting, but real experience often teaches lessons that a test cannot. Working on technical problems, making mistakes, and communicating with other people are all essential parts of preparing for a career. This realization motivates me to look for internships, research opportunities, and projects while continuing to take my courses seriously.

Academic growth

Connecting knowledge to practice

Through both coursework and hands-on work, I have learned that understanding an idea is different from applying it. When a prototype or program fails, I need to isolate the issue, challenge my assumptions, and test one change at a time. The same method is valuable when troubleshooting technology in a help desk role. I want to continue strengthening that deliberate problem-solving process through computer engineering projects and professional experience.

My next steps

Learning with purpose

As I continue at Georgia Tech, I want to use my time intentionally. I plan to learn from faculty and peers, contribute to projects outside ordinary assignments, and keep a record of what worked and what I would do differently. This portfolio will help me reflect on progress over time and make more informed decisions about the skills and experiences to pursue.
